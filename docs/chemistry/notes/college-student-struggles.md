# Collège (France) student struggles and what they mean for the app

Date: 2026-10-15

## Summary
The owner refocused on French collège (ages 11-15, cycle 4), 1-3 shared headsets, solo student use that also works teacher-led. The best-documented struggles are not about danger or cost but about linking what students see to the particle level. This makes the invisible particle level the strongest value of the app; the dangerous/expensive angle is secondary.

## Key points
Ranked by the expert's judgment (no single ranked source found).

1. **Linking macro (what is seen) to micro (particles).**
   - Fact (abstract only): French didactics research says collège chemistry is hard because it links macroscopic concepts with a microscopic model; if the concept of substance is not built, the concept of chemical transformation is not (Kermen, 2019).
   - Fact (abstract only): collège students read experiments through spontaneous particle models (Séré, ASTER 14).
   - Established: Johnstone's triangle (macro, particle, symbolic); novices are overloaded switching between levels.
2. **Giving particles the properties of the material** (for example "sugar particles melt"). Fact: Grades 6-11 study (CERP 2015); dissolution is the hardest to explain at particle level.
3. **Dissolving: "it disappears" and mass.** Fact (via RSC): younger learners accept that solutes disappear; an older study cited by RSC found only 40% of 12-13 year olds predicted mass is conserved on dissolving.
4. **Conservation of mass in a transformation.** Fact: heating copper carbonate loses mass because gas escapes, which confuses students (RSC 11-14 misconceptions). Open vs closed system is the key idea. Fact: the French programme places quantitative mass conservation in 5e (Eduscol).
5. **Physical vs chemical change.** Fact (abstract): macroscopic characteristics alone do not let students recognize a change of substance; a particle model does.
6. **Symbols and formulas.** Fact: students struggle to convert particle pictures into symbols (French lycée and first-year university study). Judgment: collège students probably share this; no collège-specific data found.

Programme (fact, 2015 text, may be revised): cycle 4 covers states of matter, chemical transformations (combustion, acid-base, acid-metal), pH (3e), using a given reaction equation. The split between 5e, 4e, 3e was not confirmed.

## Judgment on the app
- Solo use needs a guided script (prediction, action, immediate feedback); teacher-led mirroring then comes for free.
- Teacher review gives trust on pedagogical intent. Precision must come from sourced authored rules and honest review status, not from teacher review alone. Wording: "rules sourced and reviewed, experiment checked by the teacher", not "guaranteed accurate".
- Teenagers copy what they see: avoid experiments built on mixing household products (for example bleach with an acid releases toxic chlorine gas); show consequences stylized, without imitable quantities or steps.
- Most struggle-focused experiments are low-risk, which suits solo teen use.
- Simple animations also bridge the three levels, so VR must justify itself against them; no source shows VR improves collège outcomes.
- Technical consequence for the architect: these experiences need mass bookkeeping, open/closed systems and a particle view rather than `MixingHeat`.

## Use cases for the app
- Dissolving sugar or salt with a mass counter and a zoom to particles (struggles 2, 3).
- Heating in an open vs a closed flask with mass shown (struggle 4).
- A reaction with atoms rearranging while the macroscopic observation continues (struggles 1, 5).
