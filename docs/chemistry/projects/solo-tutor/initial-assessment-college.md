# Concept: AI tutor + VR/PC solo chemistry practice for collège

Date: 2026-10-15

## Summary
New concept from the owner: teenagers (French collège, cycle 4) practice chemistry exercises alone, at home or at school. VR if the school has headsets, otherwise PC. An AI acts as a tutor outside class hours (generates exercises from pre-programmed bricks, adapts difficulty, answers questions, gives short explanations, keeps long-term memory of the student). Not replacing teachers. Verdict (judgment): the idea is sound if correctness lives in code and the AI only talks; the "VR for home" part and the "long-term memory" part are the weakest.

## Assessment (judgment unless marked)
Strengths
- Real gap: nobody helps a 13-year-old at 8 pm on homework. Fits the owner's "teacher not there" framing.
- Code-guaranteed exercises plus AI as explainer is the right split. The AI never decides what is true.
- Gamified practice fits collège: short, concrete, visual.

Risks
- Hallucination (fact: LLMs can state wrong science confidently). Mitigation: AI only gets a fact sheet from your bricks, answers "I don't know, ask your teacher" outside it, and never judges answers (code does).
- Curriculum fit. Fact: cycle 4 physique-chimie has the theme "Organisation et transformations de la matière" (states of matter, chemical transformations, matter in the Universe). Teachers choose the order across 5e-3e. Collège is introductory and largely qualitative, so heavy formula/calculation exercises are NOT what collège needs. Pick a level (for example 5e or 3e) and map each brick to a programme line.
- VR for under-15s. Fact (2023 news, may have changed): Meta lowered the Quest minimum age to 10 with parent-managed accounts; Horizon Worlds stays restricted. Practice: most schools have 0-3 headsets, so solo VR for a class is a logistics problem (cleaning, setup, one student at a time). Judgment: also check Meta's current guidance on short sessions for children.
- Home use. Judgment: almost no student has a headset at home, so the home scenario is PC/browser only. VR is a school-only bonus. Say this honestly in the pitch.
- Evidence. Fact (earlier notes): VR gives presence/motivation, not shown better learning than desktop simulations. Gamification effect fades if the game is a quiz in disguise.
- Long-term memory, minors and GDPR. Fact: in France a minor can consent alone to online data processing from age 15; under 15, parent consent together with the child (CNIL recommendation 4, 2021). Collège students are mostly under 15. Judgment: for a portfolio, store the student profile locally (device) or pseudonymous; send no names to an AI API; no free chat logs stored by default. Not legal advice.
- Free chat with children: risk of off-topic or inappropriate answers and of dangerous "how do I make..." questions. Needs scope limits and refusal rules.
- Adoption. Judgment: teachers adopt what they can see and check (progress report, which misconception was hit). A black-box tutor is rejected. A weekly summary for the teacher is the adoption lever.
- Competition. Practice: PhET, Labster, Lumni/Pronote-linked exercise sites and French platforms exist; the edge is adaptive practice plus particle-level visuals, not "exercises" alone.

## Candidate exercise types (collège, ranked by judgment)
"Det" = can be generated by deterministic parametrized code; AI only explains.

1. Particle sorter / "zoom in" (states, mixtures, dissolving). Predict what the particles look like, then see the animation. Hits the top documented struggle (macro-micro link). Det: yes (rules per substance/state). VR: nice (walk "inside"), desktop: fully fine.
2. Mass bookkeeping puzzle (dissolving, open vs closed flask). Predict the balance reading, then run. Targets "it disappears" and mass conservation. Det: yes (simple arithmetic, open/closed flag). VR: little benefit; desktop best.
3. Physical vs chemical change classifier (melting ice, burning, dissolving, rusting). Det: yes (labelled data bank with reasons). Desktop.
4. Mixtures and separation (filter, decant, evaporate, distill): choose the right method for a given mixture. Det: yes (property table: soluble, particle size, boiling point). VR: good for handling steps; desktop fine.
5. Atoms and molecules builder (given a formula like H2O, CO2, build it; check atom counts). Rearrange atoms in a reaction so atoms are conserved. Det: yes (counting). Desktop or VR (3D grab is natural).
6. pH and acid/base sorting (3e): predict colour/pH of everyday liquids; neutralization as a slider. Det: yes. Safety: use safe, stylized examples.
7. Circuits (electricity, cycle 4 physique side): build a circuit, predict bulb/ammeter. Det: yes, with a simple solver. Strong VR/desktop fit, but it is physics, not chemistry; mention only if scope widens.
8. Safe lab routine (identify glassware, safety pictograms, goggles-before-start). Det: yes. VR: good (embodied), desktop acceptable. Pitched as pre-lab prep.

Avoid (safety, judgment): anything based on mixing household products (for example bleach plus acid gives toxic chlorine gas); show no imitable steps or quantities.

## Determinism rule (design principle)
- Brick = template + parameter ranges + a code-checked answer + a short sourced explanation + a misconception tag.
- AI: picks the next brick from the student profile, rewrites hints at the student's level, answers questions only from the bricks' fact sheet.
- Code: generates numbers, checks answers, tracks mastery.

## MVP slice for a solo portfolio piece (judgment)
- One level (for example 5e), one topic: "dissolving and conservation of mass", desktop first (Unity WebGL or PC), 3 brick types: particle zoom prediction, mass prediction (open/closed), classify physical/chemical change.
- 20-30 parametrized variants, a mastery tracker per brick type, local profile only.
- AI layer: next-brick choice plus hint/explanation from a fixed fact sheet, scoped chat with refusal; a log of which misconception tag was missed.
- One teacher-facing summary screen. VR: only the particle zoom as an optional scene, added last.
- Test with 1 real collège teacher and 2-3 teens before building more.
